## A photograph is evidence only when its location is known

On a long viaduct, two similar-looking piers can be far apart. Before recording a crack, establish the bridge identifier, carriageway, span or pier number, viewing direction, and component face. Use the same location system in photographs, field notes, drawings, and later reports. This simple discipline helps another engineer revisit an observation without relying on the original inspector's memory.

For road bridges in India, **IRC:SP:52**, the Bridge Inspector's Reference Manual, is a useful reference for bridge components, visible defects, inspection reporting, equipment, and inspector safety. The [IRC-authored manual](https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/irc/irc.gov.in.sp.052.1999.pdf) supports systematic recording; the owning authority's applicable inspection requirements still govern the assignment.

## Prepare a route through the structure

Plan safe access and traffic arrangements before the visit. A suggested field sequence is the approaches and deck, followed by accessible superstructure, bearings, substructure, and waterway observations. Adapt it to the bridge and authorised access; it is not a prescribed universal inspection order.

Record inaccessible areas explicitly. A drone image, a distant photograph, and a close visual observation provide different levels of information. Do not describe a concealed surface as inspected merely because its surrounding component appears in an image. Specialist underwater, rope-access, or traffic-side work requires appropriate personnel and approved arrangements.

## Build a useful defect record

| Record field | Example of the information needed |
| --- | --- |
| Location | Bridge ID, span or pier, component, face, and viewing direction |
| Observation | What is visible, without assuming the cause |
| Extent | Measured dimensions where safely obtainable, with units |
| Evidence | Overview image, close-up, scale, date, and field-note reference |
| Follow-up | Clarification, closer access, testing, or engineering review needed |

Use an overview photograph to establish context and a closer image to show the observation. Keep the original files. Perspective, shadows, stains, vegetation, and image compression can make a feature appear different from its actual condition. A line detected by an image-processing algorithm is not automatically a structural crack.

## Account for the Indian inspection season

For a river bridge in a monsoon-influenced area, note the season, recent rainfall information available to the team, and observable waterway conditions. Compare with earlier records where possible. Blocked drainage, water staining, deposited debris, and changes around accessible protection works are useful observations, but photographs alone do not establish scour depth or foundation capacity.

As a planning practice, discuss whether visits around the monsoon or after an unusual event would answer an identified question. The frequency and any special-inspection triggers must follow the authority's applicable requirements; this article does not invent a universal IRC timetable.

## Separate observation from diagnosis

Write “water staining beneath the joint” before proposing why it exists. If a mechanism is uncertain, identify the evidence needed to distinguish alternatives. Refer questions requiring testing to the appropriate investigation plan and applicable BIS methods; visual inspection is not a substitute for material testing or structural analysis.

Conclude with a traceable observation register, access limitations, and clearly assigned follow-up actions. This makes inspection repeatable and useful for maintenance rather than just producing another folder of photographs.

> Educational field-recording guidance, not an inspection authorisation or safety certification. Use trained personnel and the bridge owner's current procedures.
